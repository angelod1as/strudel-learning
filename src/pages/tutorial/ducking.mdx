## What ducking is

In a DAW you would put a compressor on the bass and feed the kick into its sidechain input, so that
every kick squashes the bass for a moment. The result is the pumping you hear in house, techno and
most modern pop.

Strudel skips the compressor entirely. **A pattern can directly duck an orbit.** The kick pattern
says "whenever I hit, turn orbit 2 down", and that is the whole mechanism.

<Box>
Because ducking targets an **orbit**, <L to="/tutorial/orbits">the orbit chapter</L> is a prerequisite. The
thing you duck is a bus, not a pattern.
</Box>

## The minimal example

Two patterns. The bass is put on orbit 2; the kick ducks orbit 2.

<Strudel
  code={`$: n(run(16)).scale("c:minor:pentatonic").s("sawtooth").delay(.7).orbit(2)

$: s("bd:4!4").beat("0,4,8,11,14",16).duckorbit(2).duckattack(0.2).duckdepth(1)`}
  label="the canonical ducking patch"
/>

<Box kind="try">
Delete `.duckorbit(2)` and re-evaluate. The two patterns still play, but they now sit on top of
each other and the bass masks the kick. Put it back. That is the entire point of the effect.
</Box>

Note what the ducking pattern is: a normal, audible kick. It does two jobs at once — you hear it,
*and* it ducks. If you only want the ducking and not the sound, silence it with `postgain(0)`:

<Strudel
  code={`$: n(run(16)).scale("c:minor:pentatonic").s("sawtooth").delay(.7).orbit(2)

$: s("bd*4").duckorbit(2).duckattack(0.3).duckonset(0.01).postgain(0)`}
  label="an inaudible ducker — a pure sidechain trigger"
/>

## The four controls

| Control | Aliases | Range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `duckorbit` | `duck` | orbit number(s) | which orbit(s) to duck |
| `duckdepth` | — | 0…1 | how far down it goes |
| `duckonset` | `duckons` | seconds | time to **reach** the lowest volume |
| `duckattack` | `duckatt`, `datt` | seconds | time to **return** to normal volume |

<Box kind="warn">
The names are counter-intuitive if you come from compressors. `duckonset` is the *downward* ramp
(what a compressor calls attack) and `duckattack` is the *recovery* (what a compressor calls
release). Read them as: onset = going down, attack = coming back.
</Box>

### duckdepth — how much

<Strudel
  code={`stack(
  n(run(8)).scale("c:minor").s("sawtooth").delay(.7).orbit(2),
  s("bd:4!4").beat("0,4,8,11,14",16).duckorbit(2).duckattack(0.2).duckdepth("<1 .9 .6 0>")
)`}
  label="full duck, then less, then off"
/>

Depth 1 is a complete gate. Depth 0.3–0.6 is what most records actually use — enough to feel the
pump, not enough to notice the gap.

### duckattack — how fast it comes back

This is the control that decides the *genre*.

<Strudel
  code={`$: n(run(8)).scale("c:minor").s("sawtooth").delay(.7).orbit(2)

$: s("bd:4!4").beat("0,4,8,11,14",16).duckorbit(2)
   .duckattack("<0.2 0 0.4>").duckdepth(1)`}
  label="0.2 = normal pump, 0 = instant (barely audible), 0.4 = long swell"
/>

Short recovery (0.05–0.1) reads as tightness. Long recovery (0.3–0.5) reads as a breathing,
building pad. At 0 the duck is over before you perceive it.

### duckonset — how fast it goes down, and clicks

Setting the onset to exactly 0 means an instantaneous volume jump, which is a click:

<Strudel
  code={`$: freq("63.2388").s("sine").orbit(2).gain(4)

$: s("bd*4").duckorbit(2).duckattack(0.3).duckonset(0).postgain(0)`}
  label="listen for the click at the start of each duck"
/>

A tiny onset removes it:

<Strudel
  code={`$: freq("63.2388").s("sine").orbit(2).gain(4)

$: s("bd*4").duckorbit(2).duckattack(0.3).duckonset(0.01).postgain(0)`}
  label="0.01s onset — no click"
/>

<Box>
Rule: `duckonset(0.01)` or thereabouts is the safe default. Set it to 0 only if you want the click
as a percussive artefact.
</Box>

A **long** onset is not a mistake to avoid — it is a rhythmic effect in its own right. The volume
slides down slowly rather than dropping:

<Strudel
  code={`$: s("pink").distort("2:1").orbit(4)
$: s("hh*16").orbit(7)
$: s("bd*4").bank("tr909").duckorbit("4:7").duckonset("0.3:0.003").duckattack(0.25)`}
  label="noise swells down slowly; hats duck instantly"
/>

## Ducking several orbits at once

`duckorbit` accepts a colon-separated list, and every other duck control can then take a matching
list — one value per orbit.

<Strudel
  code={`$: n(run(16)).scale("c:minor:pentatonic").s("sawtooth").delay(.7).orbit(2)
$: s("hh*16").orbit(3)

$: s("bd:4!4").beat("0,4,8,11,14",16).duckorbit("2:3").duckattack(0.2).duckdepth(1)`}
  label="one kick ducking both the bass and the hats"
/>

Different amounts per orbit — duck the bass hard, the hats lightly:

<Strudel
  code={`$: n(run(16)).scale("c:minor:pentatonic").s("sawtooth").delay(.7).orbit(2)
$: s("hh*16").orbit(3)

$: s("bd:4!4").beat("0,4,8,11,14",16).duckorbit("2:3").duckattack(0.2).duckdepth("1:0.5")`}
  label="depth 1 on orbit 2, depth 0.5 on orbit 3"
/>

Different recovery times per orbit:

<Strudel
  code={`$: n(run(8)).scale("c:minor").s("sawtooth").delay(.7).orbit(2)
$: s("hh*16").orbit(5)

$: s("bd:4!4").beat("0,4,8,11,14",16).duckorbit("2:5").duckattack("0.4:0.1")`}
  label="the bass breathes slowly, the hats snap back"
/>

<Box kind="warn">
The per-orbit list syntax only works if you targeted multiple orbits in the first place. Writing
`duckdepth("1:0.5")` with a single `duckorbit(2)` will not do anything useful.
</Box>

## Ducking is continuous

Ducking is one of the few effects that genuinely varies **during** a sounding note (along with the
ADSR envelopes, `tremolo`, `phaser` and `vib`). That is why it works on a long held pad, where a
pattern-level `gain` would not:

<Strudel
  code={`$: note("<c2 eb2>").s("supersaw").sustain(1).lpf(900).room(.4).orbit(2)

$: s("bd*4").bank("RolandTR909").duckorbit(2).duckdepth(.8).duckattack(.25).duckonset(.01)`}
  label="one note per two cycles, pumping four times a cycle"
/>

## Recipes

### The house / techno pump

<Strudel
  code={`setcpm(126/4)

bass: note("<c2 c2 eb2 g1>*4").s("sawtooth").lpf(500).lpq(4)
  .decay(.2).sustain(0).orbit(2)

pad: n("<0 3 5 3>").scale("C4:minor").s("gm_pad_warm")
  .attack(.4).release(1.2).room(.8).gain(.45).orbit(3)

drums: s("bd*4").bank("RolandTR909")
  .duckorbit("2:3").duckdepth("1:0.6").duckattack("0.18:0.3").duckonset(0.01)

top: s("[~ hh]*4, ~ cp").bank("RolandTR909").gain(.6).orbit(4)`}
  label="kick ducks bass hard and pad gently; hats and clap untouched"
/>

### Ducking without a kick on the beat

The ducker does not have to be a kick, or even on the downbeat. Any pattern can be the trigger:

<Strudel
  code={`$: n("0 .. 15").scale("c:minor:pentatonic").s("sawtooth").delay(.6).orbit(2)

$: s("rim").beat("2,6,10,14",16).duckorbit(2).duckdepth(.7)
   .duckattack(.1).duckonset(.005).postgain(0)`}
  label="an offbeat, silent ducker — a rhythm made of absence"
/>

### Tremolo via ducking

Put a fast silent ducker on an orbit and you have a gate:

<Strudel
  code={`$: note("<c2 eb2>").s("supersaw").sustain(1).lpf(800).orbit(2)

$: s("bd*16").duckorbit(2).duckdepth(1).duckattack(.03).duckonset(.003).postgain(0)`}
  label="16th-note gating"
/>

<QA q="duckorbit, tremolo, or an lfo on gain — which gate should I use?">
`duckorbit` when the gate should follow a **rhythm**, and especially when the trigger is a real
sound in the mix — it is the only one that is genuinely rhythmic and that acts on a whole bus.
`tremolo` when you want a smooth, continuous wobble on one voice. `lfo({c:'gain', dc:-1})` when you
need an unusual shape, skew or curve. For sidechain, always `duckorbit`.
</QA>

<QA q="Can a pattern duck its own orbit?">
Yes, and it will duck itself — usually not what you want. Keep the ducker on a different orbit
from its target.
</QA>

## Recap

| Control | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `duckorbit(n)` / `duck` | which orbit(s) this pattern ducks |
| `duckorbit("2:3")` | duck several orbits |
| `duckdepth(0…1)` | how far down — 1 is a full gate |
| `duckonset(s)` | time to go down; ~0.01 avoids clicks |
| `duckattack(s)` | time to come back — this sets the feel |
| `"a:b"` on any of them | per-orbit values, in the same order |
| `postgain(0)` | make the ducker silent but still ducking |
