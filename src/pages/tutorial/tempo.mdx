## CPS, not BPM

Tempo in Strudel is **cycles per second**, default `0.5` — one cycle every two seconds.

<Strudel code={`s("bd")`} label="0.5 cps: one kick every 2s" />

<Strudel code={`s("bd bd bd bd")`} label="still 0.5 cps — but it feels like 120 bpm" />

Nothing about the tempo changed between those two. All that changed is how many events you packed
into the cycle. Perceived tempo is a property of the content, not the clock:

<Strudel code={`s("bd hh bd hh")`} label="same cps again — feels slower, because hats read as offbeats" />

## Setting a tempo you can talk about

`setcpm(x)` sets cycles per **minute**. The useful formula is:

<Box>
`setcpm(bpm / beatsPerCycle)`

where `beatsPerCycle` is how many beats *you* hear in one cycle.
</Box>

So a 4-on-the-floor cycle at 128 bpm:

<Strudel
  code={`setcpm(128/4)
s("bd*4, [~ cp]*2, hh*8").bank("RolandTR909")`}
  label="128 bpm, 4 beats per cycle"
/>

Two beats per cycle at the same bpm:

<Strudel
  code={`setcpm(128/2)
s("bd sd, hh*4").bank("RolandTR909")`}
/>

`setcps(x)` does the same thing in cycles per second. `setcpm(x)` is `setcps(x/60)`.

<Box kind="try">
Take the 128 bpm example and change `128/4` to just `128`. It becomes absurdly fast — you asked
for 128 *cycles* per minute, and each cycle holds four kicks.
</Box>

## There are no bars

Strudel does not know what a bar is. If you want four bars of something, you write four things in
angle brackets:

<Strudel
  code={`setcpm(110/4)
s(\`<
[bd sd bd rim, hh*8]
[bd sd bd rim*2, hh*8]
>\`)`}
  label="two 'bars' as two cycles"
/>

But usually you should not. The same music, expressed as a pattern rather than a list:

<Strudel
  code={`setcpm(110/2)
s("bd <sd rim*<1 2>>, hh*4")`}
  label="the same idea, without redundancy"
/>

<Box>
Writing bars out is transcription. Nesting `< >` inside `< >` is composition. Strudel rewards the
second: variation comes from nesting alternation at different rates, not from writing more bars.
</Box>

## Time signatures

A time signature is just "how many things in the cycle". Seven:

<Strudel code={`s("bd ~ rim bd bd rim ~")`} />

Five:

<Strudel code={`s("bd hh hh bd hh, rim ~ ~ ~ ~")`} />

To switch signatures while keeping the pulse steady, weight the cycles with `@`:

<Strudel
  code={`setcpm(110*2)
s(\`<
[bd hh rim]@3
[bd hh rim sd]@4
>\`)`}
  label="3/4 then 4/4, constant beat length"
/>

Drop the `@` weights and the beat itself stretches — this is **metric modulation**:

<Strudel
  code={`setcpm(110/2)
s(\`<
[bd hh rim]
[bd hh rim sd]
>\`)`}
  label="three and four share the same cycle: the pulse changes"
/>

## Changing speed after the fact

`fast` and `slow` scale the time of a whole pattern. They are the function twins of `*` and `/`.

<Strudel code={`s("bd*4, ~ rim ~ cp").slow(2)`} />

Because arguments are patterns, the speed can itself be patterned:

<Strudel code={`s("bd*4, ~ rim ~ cp").fast("<1 [2 4]>")`} />

Stacking several speeds at once gives you phasing for free:

<Strudel code={`note("c2, eb3 g3 [bb3 c4]").s("piano").slow("0.5,1,1.5")`} label="three tempos of one pattern" />

## Nudging: early and late

Move a pattern in time without changing its speed. Values are in cycles.

<Strudel code={`$: s("bd*4")
$: s("hh*4").late(0.02).gain(.7)`} label="hats dragging just behind the kick" />

Tiny values are a groove tool; large values reorder the music.

## Swing

`swingBy(amount, subdivisions)` splits the cycle into `n` slices and pushes the second half of each
slice late. `swing(n)` is `swingBy(1/3, n)`.

<Strudel code={`s("hh*8").swingBy(1/3, 4)`} />

<Strudel code={`$: s("hh*8").swing(4)
$: s("bd ~ sd ~")`} label="swung hats over a straight beat" />

## Looping a slice of time: ribbon

`ribbon(offset, cycles)` takes `cycles` cycles starting at `offset` and loops just that. It turns
an evolving or random pattern into a fixed, repeatable riff:

<Strudel code={`n(irand(8).segment(4)).scale("c:pentatonic").ribbon(1337, 2)`} label="freeze 2 cycles of randomness" />

<Strudel code={`s("bd!16?").ribbon(29,.5)`} label="a rhythm generator: keep re-rolling the offset" />

<Box kind="try">
Change `1337` to any other number and re-evaluate. You are browsing an infinite library of
two-cycle riffs. When you find one you like, the number is the name of the riff.
</Box>

<QA q="What is the difference between fast(2) and fastGap(2)?">
`fast(2)` plays the pattern twice per cycle. `fastGap(2)` plays it once, compressed into the first
half, leaving the rest silent. Compare `s("bd sd").fast(2)` with `s("bd sd").fastGap(2)`.
</QA>

## Recap

| Function | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `setcpm(bpm/bpc)` | set tempo in cycles per minute |
| `setcps(x)` | set tempo in cycles per second |
| `fast` / `slow` | scale pattern speed |
| `early` / `late` | nudge in time |
| `swing` / `swingBy` | shuffle feel |
| `ribbon(offset, n)` | loop a window of cycles |
| `fastGap` | speed up but leave a gap |
