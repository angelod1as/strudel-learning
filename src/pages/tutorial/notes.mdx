Four spellings, one job: telling Strudel which pitch to play. You will type all four, and the
differences between them will be obvious long before the end of the chapter.

## note()

`note()` accepts note names and MIDI numbers, interchangeably.

<Exercise id="notes/note-names" title="Letters, numbers, octaves"
  goal="Say the same four notes two ways, then move them around and bend one out of tune."
  start={`note("c e g b").s("piano")`}
  target={`note("60 60.5 61 61.5").s("sawtooth")`}>
  <Step check={{ code: "\\b48\\b", match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`note("48 52 55 59").s("piano")`}
        hints={['A note name with no octave means octave 3.', 'The four MIDI numbers are a major seventh chord spelled out: root, +4, +7, +11.']}
        solution={`note("48 52 55 59").s("piano")`}>
    Replace the letters with `48 52 55 59`. Identical pitches: a bare note name has no octave, and
    the octave defaults to **3**, so `c` is MIDI 48.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'b5', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`note("c2 e3 g4 b5").s("piano")`}
        hints={['The octave digit goes straight after the letter, no space.']}
        solution={`note("c2 e3 g4 b5").s("piano")`}>
    Spread them out: `c2 e3 g4 b5`. A note name is a letter `a`–`g`, then optional accidentals,
    then an optional octave.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'eb', events: 5 }}
        hints={['`b` after the letter means flat, `#` means sharp.', 'Five notes, so five tokens.']}
        solution={`note("db eb gb ab bb").s("piano")`}>
    Now the black keys: `db eb gb ab bb`. The `b` is the accidental — `bb` really is B flat, not
    two Bs.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: '60.5', events: 4 }}
        hints={['Four MIDI numbers, two of them with a decimal point.', 'A semitone is 1, so half of one is a quarter tone.']}
        solution={`note("60 60.5 61 61.5").s("piano")`}>
    Numbers take decimals: `60 60.5 61 61.5`. Microtonality for free, and there is no way to write
    that with letters.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'sawtooth', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        hints={['Only the argument of `.s()` changes.']}
        solution={`note("60 60.5 61 61.5").s("sawtooth")`}>
    Change `.s("piano")` to `.s("sawtooth")`. A sampled piano cannot bend, so it rounds the quarter
    tones off; a synth plays them.
  </Step>
</Exercise>

<Box>
MIDI 60 is middle C, which in Strudel's numbering is `c4` — `note("60")` and `note("c4")` are the
same pitch. When in doubt, use numbers: they are unambiguous, and they can be added to.
</Box>

  start={`note("c3 eb3 g3 bb3").s("piano")`}
  target={`note("48 51 55 58.5").s("piano")`}
  check={{ includes: '58.5', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}

          'A semitone is 1. Count the minor third, the fifth and the minor seventh up from the root.',
          'A quarter tone up is half a semitone.']} />

## note vs sound

`note` sets pitch, `s` sets timbre. They are independent, and either can be left out.

<Exercise id="notes/note-vs-sound" title="Pitch and timbre are separate"
  goal="Strip the sound off a melody, put a different one on, then play two at once."
  start={`note("48 67 63 [62, 58]").s("piano")`}
  target={`note("48 67 63 [62, 58]").s("piano, gm_electric_guitar_muted")`}>
  <Step check={{ excludes: '.s(' }}
        hints={['Delete everything from the dot onwards.']}
        solution={`note("48 67 63 [62, 58]")`}>
    Delete `.s("piano")`. It still plays: with a `note` and no `s`, the default sound is
    `triangle`.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'gm_electric_guitar_muted', events: 5 }}
        hints={['Chain `.s(...)` back on with a different instrument name.']}
        solution={`note("48 67 63 [62, 58]").s("gm_electric_guitar_muted")`}>
    Add `.s("gm_electric_guitar_muted")`. Same notes, same rhythm, completely different music.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'piano,', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        hints={['A comma inside the quotes stacks, here two instruments instead of two notes.']}
        solution={`note("48 67 63 [62, 58]").s("piano, gm_electric_guitar_muted")`}>
    `s` takes mini-notation too: `.s("piano, gm_electric_guitar_muted")`. Two instruments doubling
    the same line.
  </Step>
</Exercise>

Which side of the chain you write the notes on decides the rhythm.

<Exercise id="notes/structure-from-left" title="Which side owns the rhythm"
  goal="Find out why the same three notes sometimes produce one event and sometimes three."
  start={`s("piano").note("c e g")`}
  target={`note("c e g").s("piano")`}>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'piano*3', events: 3 }}
        hints={['The left-hand pattern has exactly one slot, so it makes exactly one event.',
                '`*3` gives that slot three.']}
        solution={`s("piano*3").note("c e g")`}>
    Play it first: **one** note per cycle, not three. Now change `s("piano")` to `s("piano*3")`.
    Three events, and the note pattern is sampled at each of them.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ events: 6 }}
        hints={['Six events sampling a three-note pattern gives each note twice.']}
        solution={`s("piano*6").note("c e g")`}>
    Make it `s("piano*6")`. Six events: `c c e e g g`. The `note` pattern never adds an event of
    its own; it only supplies a value when asked.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ excludes: '*', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        hints={['Swap the two calls around.']}
        solution={`note("c e g").s("piano")`}>
    Now the natural way round: `note("c e g").s("piano")`. Three notes, because now the notes are
    on the left.
  </Step>
</Exercise>

<Box>
**Rhythm comes from the left.** `note("c e g").s("piano")` has three events;
`s("piano").note("c e g")` has one. Everything you chain on afterwards — `s`, `gain`, `lpf` — is
sampled at the moments the left-hand pattern already decided.
</Box>

<Exercise id="notes/structure-challenge" title="Challenge: one note per cycle"
  goal="Exactly one piano event per cycle, whose pitch is c3, then eb3, then g3."
  start={`note("c3 eb3 g3").s("piano")`}
  target={`s("piano").note("<c3 eb3 g3>")`}
  check={{ match: { keys: ['note', 's'] }, cycles: 3 }}
  hints={['Three events per cycle is what you are trying to avoid, so the notes cannot be on the left.',
          '`< >` hands out one element per cycle.',
          'Put the sound on the left and let it ask the notes for a value.']} />

## n() + scale()

Remembering which notes are in a key is work. `scale()` does it: `n` becomes a **scale degree**,
zero-indexed, and every number sounds right.

<Exercise id="notes/scale-degrees" title="Numbers that are always in key"
  goal="Rewrite a melody as degrees, change the mode under it, then move the key."
  start={`note("c e g b").s("piano")`}
  target={`n("<0 -3>, 2 4 <[6,8] [7,9]>")
  .scale("<C:major D:mixolydian>/4")
  .s("piano")`}>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'scale(', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`n("0 2 4 6").scale("C:major").s("piano")`}
        hints={['`n` takes the degree numbers, `scale` takes a `root:type` string.',
                'Degrees are zero-indexed, so the root is 0.']}
        solution={`n("0 2 4 6").scale("C:major").s("piano")`}>
    Replace `note("c e g b")` with `n("0 2 4 6")` and chain `.scale("C:major")`. Exactly the same
    four pitches — degrees 0, 2, 4 and 6 of C major are c, e, g and b.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'C:minor', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`n("0 2 4 6").scale("C:minor").s("piano")`}
        hints={['Only the scale name changes; the numbers stay.']}
        solution={`n("0 2 4 6").scale("C:minor").s("piano")`}>
    Change the scale to `C:minor`. The same numbers, a different mode, no wrong notes. That is why
    this is the fastest way to sketch a melody: you cannot play out of key.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: '<[6,8]', cycles: 2, events: 10 }}
        hints={['A comma inside brackets stacks two degrees at the same moment.',
                '`< >` alternates between the two stacks, one per cycle.']}
        solution={`n("0 2 4 <[6,8] [7,9]>").scale("C:minor").s("piano")`}>
    Make the last slot alternate between two stacked degrees: `n("0 2 4 <[6,8] [7,9]>")`. Nothing
    about stacking or alternating changes just because the numbers mean degrees.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'D:mixolydian', cycles: 8, match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`n("0 2 4 <[6,8] [7,9]>")
  .scale("<C:major D:mixolydian>/4")
  .s("piano")`}
        hints={['The scale argument is a pattern like any other.',
                '`/4` after the closing `>` holds each value for four cycles.']}
        solution={`n("0 2 4 <[6,8] [7,9]>")
  .scale("<C:major D:mixolydian>/4")
  .s("piano")`}>
    Move the key under the melody: `.scale("<C:major D:mixolydian>/4")`. Four cycles in one scale,
    four in the other, and the degrees never change.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: '<0 -3>', cycles: 8, match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        hints={['A comma at the top level of the `n` pattern adds a layer.',
                'Negative degrees go below the root.']}
        solution={`n("<0 -3>, 2 4 <[6,8] [7,9]>")
  .scale("<C:major D:mixolydian>/4")
  .s("piano")`}>
    Add a slow bass part inside the same `n`: `n("<0 -3>, 2 4 <[6,8] [7,9]>")`. Negative numbers go
    below the root, and the modulation carries it along too.
  </Step>
</Exercise>

The scale name is `root:type`, with the root defaulting to octave 3:

| Example | |
| --- | --- |
| `"C:major"` `"A2:minor"` | the usual suspects |
| `"D:dorian"` `"G:mixolydian"` | modes |
| `"A2:minor:pentatonic"` | multi-word scale names joined by `:` |
| `"C:ritusen"` `"C:major:blues"` | anything [tonaljs](https://github.com/tonaljs/tonal) knows |

<Box kind="warn">
Scale names must not contain spaces inside double quotes — `"C major"` would be read as a
*sequence of two things*. Use `"C:major"`, or single quotes: `'C major'`.
</Box>

<Exercise id="notes/scale-challenge" title="Challenge: a pentatonic bass riff"
  goal="The degrees 0 3 2 4 2 1 0 played on gm_acoustic_bass, in A minor pentatonic, low enough to be a bass — root in octave 2."
  start={`n("0 3 2 4 2 1 0").s("gm_acoustic_bass")`}
  target={`n("0 3 2 4 2 1 0").scale("A2:minor:pentatonic").s("gm_acoustic_bass")`}
  check={{ match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
  hints={['Without a scale the numbers are not pitches at all, so one function is missing.',
          'The root of a scale carries its own octave number.',
          'Multi-word scale types are joined with colons, not spaces.']} />

## Transposing

`add()` shifts numbers. On a `note` pattern it adds semitones; on an `n` pattern inside a scale it
adds **scale degrees**, which keeps you in key.

<Exercise id="notes/add" title="Semitones, then degrees"
  goal="Move a three-note figure chromatically, then diatonically, then stack it into chords."
  start={`note("c3 e3 g3").s("piano")`}
  target={`n("0 2 4 6".add("<0 [0,2,4]>")).scale("C:major").s("piano")`}>
  <Step check={{ includes: '.add(', cycles: 4, match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`note("c3 e3 g3".add("<0 5 7 0>")).s("piano")`}
        hints={['`add` goes on the string, inside `note(...)`.',
                'One value per cycle, so `< >`.']}
        solution={`note("c3 e3 g3".add("<0 5 7 0>")).s("piano")`}>
    Put the transposition on the string itself:
    `note("c3 e3 g3".add("<0 5 7 0>"))`. On a `note` pattern the numbers are **semitones**, so
    that is up a fourth, up a fifth, and back.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: ['n(', 'scale('], cycles: 4, match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`n("0 2 4 6".add("<0 3 4 0>")).scale("C:major").s("piano")`}
        hints={['Same shape, but `n` instead of `note` and a `.scale()` on the end.',
                'Inside a scale, 3 means three steps of the scale, not three semitones.']}
        solution={`n("0 2 4 6".add("<0 3 4 0>")).scale("C:major").s("piano")`}>
    Now inside a scale: `n("0 2 4 6".add("<0 3 4 0>")).scale("C:major")`. The added numbers are
    now **degrees**, so the transposition stays in key — thirds and fourths, never a wrong note.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: '[0,2,4]', cycles: 2, events: 16 }}
        hints={['Stacking is a comma, and it works inside an `add` just as it works anywhere else.',
                'One cycle plain, one cycle stacked.']}
        solution={`n("0 2 4 6".add("<0 [0,2,4]>")).scale("C:major").s("piano")`}>
    Change the add to `"<0 [0,2,4]>"`. Adding three things at once to every note is how you stack a
    chord: plain for one cycle, then every note becomes a triad.
  </Step>
</Exercise>

<Exercise id="notes/add-challenge" title="Challenge: a xylophone figure"
  goal="Degrees 0 [2 4] <3 5> [~ <4 1>] in C5 minor on gm_xylophone with room 0.5, played plain on one cycle and stacked with degrees 0, 2 and 4 on the next."
  start={`n("0 2 4").scale("C5:minor").s("gm_xylophone")`}
  target={`n("0 [2 4] <3 5> [~ <4 1>]".add("<0 [0,2,4]>")).scale("C5:minor").s("gm_xylophone").room(.5)`}
  check={{ match: { keys: ['note', 's', 'room'] }, cycles: 4 }}
  hints={['The alternating stack is the same `add` trick you just used.',
          'The degree string goes inside `n(...)` and the `add` wraps the string, not the `n`.',
          '`room` is a chained control, like `s`.']} />

Two more precise tools come from the tonal package.

<Exercise id="notes/transpose" title="transpose and scaleTranspose"
  goal="Move a bass figure by semitones, then move a scale figure by scale steps."
  start={`"[c2 c3]*4".note()`}
  target={`"[-8 [2,4,6]]*2"
  .scale('C4:bebop:major')
  .scaleTranspose("<0 -1 -2 -3 -4 -5 -6 -4>*2")
  .note()`}>
  <Step check={{ includes: '.transpose(', cycles: 4, match: { keys: ['note'] } }}
        target={`"[c2 c3]*4".transpose("<0 -2 5 3>").note()`}
        hints={['It goes between the string and `.note()`.',
                'Numbers are semitones; strings like `3M` or `5P` are interval names.']}
        solution={`"[c2 c3]*4".transpose("<0 -2 5 3>").note()`}>
    Add `.transpose("<0 -2 5 3>")` before `.note()`. One transposition per cycle, counted in
    semitones — or in interval names such as `3M` and `5P` if you prefer to read them.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'bebop', excludes: 'transpose' }}
        hints={['The numbers become scale degrees, so the string is numbers now, not note names.',
                'Single quotes are the other way to write a scale name.']}
        solution={`"[-8 [2,4,6]]*2"
  .scale('C4:bebop:major')
  .note()`}>
    Different starting point. Replace the whole thing with `"[-8 [2,4,6]]*2".scale('C4:bebop:major').note()`:
    a low root followed by a three-note stack, twice a cycle.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: '.scaleTranspose(', cycles: 4, match: { keys: ['note'] } }}
        hints={['It goes after `.scale(...)` — it needs to know the scale to count steps in.',
                'Eight values, stepping down, twice as fast as the cycle.']}
        solution={`"[-8 [2,4,6]]*2"
  .scale('C4:bebop:major')
  .scaleTranspose("<0 -1 -2 -3 -4 -5 -6 -4>*2")
  .note()`}>
    Insert `.scaleTranspose("<0 -1 -2 -3 -4 -5 -6 -4>*2")` after the scale. It walks the whole
    figure down the scale a step at a time, and every voice stays in key.
  </Step>
</Exercise>

  goal="A two-cycle bass: c2 g2 c2 g2 on gm_acoustic_bass, repeated a fifth lower on the second cycle."
  start={`"c2 g2 c2 g2".note().s("gm_acoustic_bass")`}
  target={`"c2 g2 c2 g2".transpose("<0 -7>").note().s("gm_acoustic_bass")`}
  check={{ match: { keys: ['note', 's'] }, cycles: 2 }}

          'A perfect fifth is seven semitones, and down is negative.',
          'One value per cycle means `< >`.']} />

## Chords and voicings

Write the symbol and let Strudel choose the notes.

<Exercise id="notes/voicing" title="Chord symbols to actual notes"
  goal="Replace sixteen hand-written notes with four chord symbols, then steer how they are voiced."
  start={`note("<[c3,e3,g3,b3] [a2,c3,e3,g3] [d3,f3,a3,c4] [g2,b2,d3,f3]>").s("piano")`}
  target={`chord("<C^7 Am7 Dm7 G7>").dict('lefthand').mode("root:g2").voicing().s("piano")`}>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'voicing(', cycles: 4, events: [8, 20] }}
        hints={['`chord()` takes the symbols; `voicing()` turns them into notes.',
                'Four symbols, one per cycle, so they go in `< >`.',
                'A major seventh is written `C^7`, a minor seventh `Am7`, a dominant `G7`.']}
        solution={`chord("<C^7 Am7 Dm7 G7>").voicing().s("piano")`}>
    Replace all of it with `chord("<C^7 Am7 Dm7 G7>").voicing().s("piano")`. Four symbols instead
    of sixteen notes, and `voicing()` picks an inversion that does not leap around.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: "dict(", cycles: 4, match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`chord("<C^7 Am7 Dm7 G7>").dict('lefthand').voicing().s("piano")`}
        hints={['It is a control, so it goes before `voicing()` — that is what reads it.',
                'The default dictionary is `ireal`; the others are `lefthand`, `triads` and `guidetones`.']}
        solution={`chord("<C^7 Am7 Dm7 G7>").dict('lefthand').voicing().s("piano")`}>
    Swap the voicing dictionary: add `.dict('lefthand')` before `.voicing()`. Same chords, a jazz
    pianist's left hand instead of the default `ireal` shapes. A dictionary only knows the
    qualities it was written for, so `lefthand` wants sevenths, not bare triads.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'anchor(', cycles: 4, match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`chord("<C^7 Am7 Dm7 G7>").dict('lefthand').anchor("c5").mode("below").voicing().s("piano")`}
        hints={['Two controls: one names the reference note, the other says which side of it to sit on.',
                'They both go before `voicing()`.']}
        solution={`chord("<C^7 Am7 Dm7 G7>").dict('lefthand').anchor("c5").mode("below").voicing().s("piano")`}>
    Pin it to a register: add `.anchor("c5").mode("below")`. The anchor is the reference note and
    `below` means the **top** note lands at or under it. `mode("above")` does the mirror image.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'root:g2', excludes: 'anchor(', cycles: 4, match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        hints={['`mode` can carry its own reference note after a colon, so the anchor is not needed.',
                'Delete `.anchor("c5")` and change what `mode` is given.']}
        solution={`chord("<C^7 Am7 Dm7 G7>").dict('lefthand').mode("root:g2").voicing().s("piano")`}>
    Replace `.anchor("c5").mode("below")` with `.mode("root:g2")`: the bottom note is now the
    chord's own root, placed above g2. The voicing has a bass in it.
  </Step>
</Exercise>

| Control | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `dict('lefthand')` | swap the voicing dictionary (`ireal` is the default; also `triads`, `guidetones`) |
| `anchor("c5")` | the reference note to align to |
| `mode("below")` | top note at or under the anchor |
| `mode("above")` | bottom note at or above the anchor |
| `mode("root:g2")` | bottom note is the chord root, above g2 |
| `offset(1)` | shift to the next voicing up |
| `n("0 1 2")` | play the voicing like a scale — an arpeggio |

<Exercise id="notes/arpeggiate-voicing" title="Play a voicing like a scale"
  goal="Turn a block chord into a guitar-like arpeggio by indexing into the voicing."
  start={`chord("<C Am F G>").voicing().s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`}
  target={`n("0 1 2 3").chord("<C Am F G>").mode("above:c3").voicing().s("gm_electric_guitar_clean").clip(2)`}>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'n("0 1 2 3")', events: 4 }}
        hints={['`n` goes at the very front, before `chord`.',
                'Index 0 is the bottom note of the voicing, 1 the next one up.']}
        solution={`n("0 1 2 3").chord("<C Am F G>").voicing().s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`}>
    Put `n("0 1 2 3")` at the front. With an `n`, `voicing()` stops stacking the notes and hands
    them out one index at a time — the voicing becomes a four-note scale.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'above:c3', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`n("0 1 2 3").chord("<C Am F G>").mode("above:c3").voicing().s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`}
        hints={['`mode` can take its reference note after a colon, so no separate anchor is needed.']}
        solution={`n("0 1 2 3").chord("<C Am F G>").mode("above:c3").voicing().s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`}>
    Add `.mode("above:c3")` before `.voicing()`, so every arpeggio starts at or above c3 instead of
    wandering between registers.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: '.clip(', has: { clip: 2 } }}
        hints={['One chained control at the very end, with a number bigger than 1.']}
        solution={`n("0 1 2 3").chord("<C Am F G>").mode("above:c3").voicing().s("gm_electric_guitar_clean").clip(2)`}>
    Add `.clip(2)` at the end: each note now lasts twice its slot, so the arpeggio rings into
    itself the way a plucked string does.
  </Step>
</Exercise>

The same chord symbols can feed a bassline at the same time.

<Exercise id="notes/rootnotes" title="A comp and a bass from one source"
  goal="Give a chord progression a rhythm, then split it into two instruments that agree by construction."
  start={`"<C^7 A7b13 Dm7 G7>*2".voicing().note().s("gm_electric_piano_1").room(.4)`}
  target={`"<C^7 A7b13 Dm7 G7>*2".layer(
  x => x.voicing().struct("[~ x]*2").note().s("gm_electric_piano_1").room(.4),
  x => x.rootNotes(2).note().s("sawtooth").lpf(800).decay(.2).sustain(0)
)`}>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'struct(', fn: "(h) => { const x = [...new Set(h.map(e => +(e.t * 4).toFixed(3)))].join(' '); return x === '1 3' || 'Chords should land on the second half of each half-cycle, i.e. quarters 1 and 3, got ' + x + '.' }" }}
        hints={['`struct` replaces the rhythm with a grid: `x` is a hit, `~` a rest.',
                'Two slots, the second one sounding, twice a cycle.']}
        solution={`"<C^7 A7b13 Dm7 G7>*2".voicing().struct("[~ x]*2").note().s("gm_electric_piano_1").room(.4)`}>
    Add `.struct("[~ x]*2")` after `.voicing()`. The chords stop being held and start being
    played: two offbeat stabs per cycle.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: '.layer(', fn: "(h) => { const x = [...new Set(h.map(e => +(e.t * 4).toFixed(3)))].join(' '); return x === '1 3' || 'The comp should be unchanged: quarters 1 and 3, got ' + x + '.' }" }}
        hints={['`layer` takes functions of the source pattern; each one becomes a lane.',
                'The whole chain after the string moves inside an arrow function.']}
        solution={`"<C^7 A7b13 Dm7 G7>*2".layer(
  x => x.voicing().struct("[~ x]*2").note().s("gm_electric_piano_1").room(.4)
)`}>
    Wrap the chain in `layer`: `"<C^7 A7b13 Dm7 G7>*2".layer(x => x.voicing().struct("[~ x]*2").note().s("gm_electric_piano_1").room(.4))`.
    It sounds identical — but now the chord symbols are a source that more than one voice can read.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'rootNotes(', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        hints={['A second arrow function after a comma.',
                '`rootNotes(octave)` turns a chord symbol into its root note — the bass player’s job.',
                'It needs its own `.note()` and its own sound.']}
        solution={`"<C^7 A7b13 Dm7 G7>*2".layer(
  x => x.voicing().struct("[~ x]*2").note().s("gm_electric_piano_1").room(.4),
  x => x.rootNotes(2).note().s("sawtooth").lpf(800).decay(.2).sustain(0)
)`}>
    Add a second lane: `x => x.rootNotes(2).note().s("sawtooth").lpf(800).decay(.2).sustain(0)`.
    `rootNotes(octave)` takes the root out of each symbol, so the bass can never disagree with the
    comp — change one symbol and both voices follow.
  </Step>
</Exercise>

## Arpeggios

Four ways to do the same thing, each suggesting different music.

<Exercise id="notes/arp" title="Four ways up a chord"
  goal="Write one arpeggio by hand, by degree, out of a stack, and out of delays."
  start={`note("c eb g c4").s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`}
  target={`"0".off(1/3, add(2)).off(1/2, add(4))
  .n().scale("C:minor").s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`}>
  <Step check={{ includes: '.clip(', has: { clip: 2 } }}
        hints={['One control at the end, letting each note outlast its slot.']}
        solution={`note("c eb g c4").clip(2).s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`}>
    **By hand.** Add `.clip(2)` so the notes overlap into a chord rather than four separate stabs.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: 'scale(', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        target={`n("0 2 4 7").scale("C:minor").clip(2).s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`}
        hints={['Root, third, fifth, octave are degrees 0, 2, 4 and 7 of a seven-note scale.']}
        solution={`n("0 2 4 7").scale("C:minor").clip(2).s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`}>
    **By scale degree.** Replace the notes with `n("0 2 4 7").scale("C:minor")`. Same arpeggio, and
    now the shape survives a change of key.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: '.arp(', cycles: 4, events: 24 }}
        hints={['The chords are stacks in `< >`, and `arp` reads indices out of whatever is stacked.',
                'An index can itself be a stack, so `[0,2]` plays two of them at once.']}
        solution={`note("<[c,eb,g]!2 [c,f,ab] [d,f,ab]>").arp("0 [0,2] 1 [0,2]").s("piano")`}>
    **Out of a stack.** Replace it all with
    `note("<[c,eb,g]!2 [c,f,ab] [d,f,ab]>").arp("0 [0,2] 1 [0,2]").s("piano")`. `arp` takes a
    chord that is already stacked and picks it apart by index.
  </Step>
  <Step check={{ includes: '.off(', match: { keys: ['note', 's'] } }}
        hints={['`off(time, fn)` copies the pattern, moves the copy later, and applies the function to it.',
                'Two copies, a third and a half of a cycle late, each transposed further up the scale.']}
        solution={`"0".off(1/3, add(2)).off(1/2, add(4))
  .n().scale("C:minor").s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`}>
    **Out of delays.** Replace it with
    `"0".off(1/3, add(2)).off(1/2, add(4)).n().scale("C:minor").s("gm_electric_guitar_clean")`.
    One note, echoed twice at different offsets and different degrees: an arpeggio made of delays.
  </Step>
</Exercise>

<QA q="When should I use note, n+scale, or chord+voicing?">
`note` when you know exactly which pitches you want. `n`+`scale` when you want to improvise shapes
and stay in key — it is the best choice while sketching. `chord`+`voicing` when you are thinking
harmonically (a progression) rather than melodically, or when you want a bassline and a comp that
agree with each other automatically.
</QA>

## Recap

| Function | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `note()` | pitch by name or MIDI number |
| `n()` + `scale()` | pitch by scale degree |
| `add()` | transpose (semitones, or degrees inside a scale) |
| `transpose()` | semitones or interval names |
| `scaleTranspose()` | move by scale steps |
| `chord()` + `voicing()` | chord symbols to notes |
| `rootNotes(oct)` | chord symbols to bass notes |
| `arp()` | play a stacked chord one index at a time |
