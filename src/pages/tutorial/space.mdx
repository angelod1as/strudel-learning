Nearly every effect in Strudel is **local**: a fresh chain of nodes is built for each event, used,
and thrown away. Delay and reverb are different. They are **global**: one delay unit and one reverb
unit exist per *orbit*, and every event in that orbit is sent into the same one.

That is what makes them sound like a room instead of a hundred separate rooms — and it is also the
source of the most common confusing bug in Strudel, which the [next chapter](/tutorial/orbits)
is about.

## Delay

<Strudel code={`s("bd bd").delay("<0 .25 .5 1>")`} label="delay level: how much goes into the delay" />

Three parameters:

| Control | Aliases | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `delay` | — | send level, 0…1 |
| `delaytime` | `delayt`, `dt` | time between repeats, in **seconds** |
| `delayfeedback` | `delayfb`, `dfb` | how much of the output goes back in, 0…1 |

<Strudel
  code={`$: note("[~ [<[d3,a3,f4]!2 [d3,bb3,g4]!2> ~]]*2")
  .s("gm_electric_guitar_muted").delay(.5)

$: s("bd rim").bank("RolandTR707").delay(.5)`}
  label="a dub guitar"
/>

All three can go in one string, separated by colons — `delay("level:time:feedback")`:

<Strudel code={`s("bd bd").delay("0.65:0.25:0.9 0.65:0.125:0.7")`} />

<Box kind="try">
Start from `s("bd rim").delay(.5)` and work up:
- `.delay(".8:.125")` — tighter repeats
- `.delay(".8:.06:.8")` — a slapback that lingers
- `delayfeedback(1)` — runaway. Keep a finger on `ctrl+.`
</Box>

<Box kind="warn">
`delayfeedback` at or above 1 means the delay gets louder forever. It will not stop when the
pattern does.
</Box>

Since `delaytime` is in seconds, it does not follow the tempo. To lock it to the grid, compute it:
at `setcps(0.5)` a cycle is 2 seconds, so an eighth of a cycle is `2/8`.

<Strudel
  code={`setcps(0.5)
note("c3 eb3 g3 bb3").s("sawtooth").decay(.1).sustain(0)
  .delay(.6).delaytime(2/8).delayfeedback(.7)`}
  label="delay locked to the cycle"
/>

`delayspeed` modulates the delay line's playback rate, giving tape-style pitch artefacts:

<Strudel
  code={`note("d d a# a".fast(2))
  .s("sawtooth")
  .delay(.8)
  .delaytime(1/2)
  .delayspeed("<2 .5 -1 -2>")`}
/>

## Reverb

<Strudel code={`s("bd sd [~ bd] sd").room("<0 .2 .4 .6 .8 1>")`} label="room: send level into the reverb" />

| Control | Aliases | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `room` | — | send level, 0…1 |
| `roomsize` | `rsize`, `sz`, `size` | size of the space, 0…10 |
| `roomfade` | — | decay time |
| `roomlp` | `rlp` | lowpass on the reverb tail, in Hz |
| `roomdim` | `rdim` | frequency at which the tail is 60 dB down |
| `iresponse` | — | use an impulse response sample instead |

<Strudel code={`s("bd sd [~ bd] sd").room(.8).rsize("<1 4 8>")`} label="from a booth to a cathedral" />

<Strudel
  code={`$: s("bd sd [~ bd] sd").room(0.5).rlp(10000)
$: s("hh*8").gain(.5)`}
  label="rlp high: bright tail"
/>

<Strudel
  code={`$: s("bd sd [~ bd] sd").room(0.5).rlp(3000).rdim(400)
$: s("hh*8").gain(.5)`}
  label="rlp low + rdim low: dark, distant tail"
/>

Level and size also fit in one string:

<Strudel code={`s("bd sd [~ bd] sd").room("<0.9:1 0.9:4>")`} />

<Box kind="warn">
`roomsize`, `roomlp`, `roomdim` and `roomfade` **rebuild the reverb** when they change. Patterning
them fast is expensive and will glitch. Pattern `room` (the send level) freely; change the shape of
the room sparingly.
</Box>

## Using them musically

Reverb and delay are how you place sounds at different distances. A useful default:

<Strudel
  code={`$: s("bd*4").bank("RolandTR909")                      // dry, up front
$: s("~ cp").bank("RolandTR909").room(.2).rsize(1)     // just behind
$: n("<4 [3@3 4] [<2 0> ~@16] ~>").scale("D4:minor")
   .s("gm_accordion:2").room(2).gain(.4)               // far away
$: note("[~ [<[d3,a3,f4]!2 [d3,bb3,g4]!2> ~]]*2")
   .s("gm_electric_guitar_muted").delay(.5)`}
  label="a little dub tune"
/>

<Box kind="try">
Add `.hush()` to the end of any one of those lines and re-evaluate to hear how much each layer was
contributing. Then remove the `room` from the accordion — the whole thing collapses to the front of
the stage.
</Box>

## The catch

Watch what happens when two patterns with different reverb settings play at once:

<Strudel
  code={`$: s("triangle*4").decay(0.5).n(irand(12)).scale('C:minor')
  .room(1).roomsize(10)

$: s("bd*4").room(0.01).roomsize(0.01).postgain(0)`}
  label="the muted kick destroys the pluck's reverb"
/>

The second pattern is silent (`postgain(0)`) and yet it changed the first one's sound. Both are on
the default orbit `1`, so they share one reverb — and the last setting wins.

The fix is one word:

<Strudel
  code={`$: s("triangle*4").decay(0.5).n(irand(12)).scale('C:minor')
  .room(1).roomsize(10).orbit(2)

$: s("bd*4").room(0.01).roomsize(0.01).postgain(0)`}
  label="separate orbits, separate reverbs"
/>

<QA q="So should I just put every pattern on its own orbit?">
No — and the next chapter explains why. Orbits are not free, and sharing one is often exactly what
you want (it is how several instruments end up sounding like they are in the same room). You want
a *deliberate* handful of them.
</QA>

## Recap

| Control | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `delay` `delaytime` `delayfeedback` | the delay send |
| `delay("lvl:time:fb")` | all three at once |
| `delayspeed` | tape-style pitch on the repeats |
| `room` `roomsize` | the reverb send |
| `roomlp` `roomdim` `roomfade` | tone and decay of the tail |
| `iresponse` | convolution with your own impulse |
